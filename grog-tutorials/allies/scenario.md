# The Willow That Came Back
### A First-Session Tutorial for the Covenant of the Hollow Crown

---

## What This Is

A one-evening tutorial for Ars Magica: Definitive Edition, using the covenant's own grogs as
player characters. The three magi stay off-stage. Their deeds caused this problem. The grogs get
to live with it.

**The hook in one sentence:** The magi drained the marsh, something woke up, and now the grogs
have to retrieve a vis-flask from under a tree that should not exist — while armed silver-merchants
are also in the valley asking questions.

**Teaching order:** Character sheet (Scene 1) → Simple Die (Scene 2) → Stress Die and botch
(Scene 3) → Full combat (Scene 4) → The world is bigger than you (Scene 5).

**Runtime:** One evening, roughly three hours.

---

## Part 1 — What to Prep

You need **four index cards**, no more.

**Card 1 — The Covenant**
*Covenant of the Hollow Crown. A converted Roman posting-house on a bluff above a newly drained
marsh, Silberbachtal valley, Black Forest, 1221 AD. The valley is conspicuously blessed and
everyone is starting to notice.*

**Card 2 — The Three Magi (off-stage)**
- *Corvus of Tytalus — scheming, wax-tablet, sent the grogs, knows more than he said*
- *Konstantinos of Bonisagus — careful, dictation-voice, is watching through the listening-stone*
- *Aldric of Flambeau — drained the marsh, proud of his twenty-two acres, caused this*

Grogs serve these men. They are not the grogs' friends. They will not be thanked.

**Card 3 — Mythic Europe in one sentence**
*It's real 13th-century Germany — real Holy Roman Emperor, real Benedictine monks — plus magic,
faeries, and things under drained marshes that are also all literally real.*

**Card 4 — The Errand**
*Retrieve one sealed clay flask (palm-sized, wax-stopped, faintly warm to the touch) buried at the
roots of the big willow at the south end of the meadow. Konstantinos drew a map. It should take
three hours. Do not disturb anything.*

That's your entire prep. One covenant. Three off-stage wizards. One sentence of history. Five grogs.

---

## The Only Dice Rule (read this aloud at the table)

> Almost everything is **Characteristic + Ability + a die, versus an Ease Factor.**
> Meet or beat it, you succeed.
>
> - **Simple Die:** roll a d10, treat 0 as 10. Used when calm and unhurried.
> - **Stress Die:** roll a d10, but **1 explodes** (reroll and double — and again if you roll
>   another 1), and **0 means danger** — roll a botch die; if *that* shows 0, something goes
>   badly wrong. Used whenever there's pressure.
>
> Ease Factors: **3** = trivial · **6** = easy · **9** = average · **12** = hard · **15** = very hard

Introduce these one at a time. Do not front-load them.

## Part 2 — Scene by Scene

---

### Scene 1 — The Courtyard at Dawn
**Rule cluster taught: the character sheet**

The grogs muster before the main gate in the grey pre-dawn. Corvus is there — if you can call his
presence a "there." He stands in the gateway with a traveling cloak and a wax tablet, and does not
look up when the grogs arrive. He finishes what he is writing, reads it back to himself, and then
recites the errand from memory, flat and fast, like a man reading a list of supplies.

> *"The meadow's south boundary. The large willow. At the roots, approximately one cubit down,
> a clay flask sealed in black wax. You will retrieve it without disturbing the willow or its
> surroundings. You will not dig more than necessary. You will return before Vespers. Any questions
> you have should be addressed to Konstantinos, whose map you will find in the satchel. Do not
> lose the satchel."*

He goes back inside. The gate closes.

**Run the introductions.** Go around the table. Each player reads their grog's name, one
Personality Trait, and their best combat or practical Ability aloud — in character, to the group.
The Grizzled Veteran grumbles that nobody mentioned the state of the path; the Berserker complains
there's nothing to hit. The Standard Soldier checks the satchel and finds Konstantinos's map, which
is meticulous, annotated in three colors of ink, and includes a note in Latin that none of them can
read. (It says: *"Do not approach the water. There may not be water. This is still the instruction."*)

**No dice yet.** Just orient the players to the sheet: Characteristics are who you are;
Abilities are what you have trained. The numbers will matter very shortly.

> **Rule taught:** What is on the sheet and how to read it. Nothing else. The magi's coldness is
> the first lesson of the setting: these are not the grogs' allies. They are employers, at best.

### Scene 2 — The Meadow Path
**Rule cluster taught: the Simple Die and Ease Factors**

The covenant's twenty-two acres of reclaimed meadow are beautiful in the early light — black loam,
green shoots of Aldric's forced hedgerow, the wooden bridge. The path to the willow skirts the
meadow's edge where the old marsh-bed meets the new ground. It rained last night.

**The ground is treacherous.** The reclaimed land's outer margin hasn't consolidated — it's soft
black peat under a skin of grass. Twice on the approach to the willow the path dips into patches
where the ground has slumped. A grog who doesn't watch their step sinks to the ankle.

Crossing each soft patch: **Athletics + Quickness vs. Ease Factor 6.** No danger, no pressure —
**Simple Die** only. Roll: Quickness + Athletics + a d10. Meet 6, you hop across. Fail, you sink to
the knee and need help pulling free (costs a round, entertains the Berserker).

At the second slump, the Standard Soldier can notice — **Perception + Awareness vs. Ease Factor 6,
Simple Die** — that the ground here is wetter than it should be. The drainage isn't holding
properly at the south end. The marsh is seeping back.

When they arrive at the willow: it is enormous, lush, and absolutely should not be here. It was
pulled out four months ago. Its trunk is as thick as a man's chest. Its roots have broken the
surface in a wide radius and the grass within the root-circle is a vivid, wrong green — too bright,
too early in the season.

The flask is visible, barely, in the crotch of a surface root near the base.

**Easy success:** let a grog simply reach in and retrieve it — **Dexterity + Athletics vs. Ease
Factor 3, Simple Die.** It comes free easily. The flask is warm through its wax seal, which is odd.
This is not the end of the problem.

> **Rule taught:** The Simple Die, the basic roll structure, and Ease Factors as a spectrum.
> Failure here is wet boots and laughter, not danger. Let someone fail extravagantly.

### Scene 3 — The Assayer's Men
**Rule cluster taught: the Stress Die and the botch**

The grogs are on the path back with the flask when they encounter three men on the meadow-bridge:
**Brother Albrecht** (the Freiburg guild's assayer, a careful middle-aged layman in a grey
travelling cloak) and his two guards, **Heinrich** and **Kaspar** (armed merchants' retainers,
not soldiers, but competent enough to be trouble). Albrecht has been on the valley's eastern ridge
since dawn with a geologist's hammer and a sample bag.

Albrecht is perfectly civil. He wishes the grogs good morning. He notes the fine meadow. He asks,
in a conversational way, whether the covenant has plans for the south boundary of the meadow,
because — he shows them a document, folded and sealed — he carries a letter of survey-right for
the valley-floor lands, issued by the crown reeve at Waldkirch.

**This is a lie.** Reeve Eckard issued no such letter. Albrecht fabricated the survey authority
because Reeve Eckard inexplicably refused to cooperate. (The grogs don't know Eckard was
*Aura of Rightful Authority*-ed into compliance.) Albrecht is bluffing, but he's calm and the
document looks real.

Now there's social pressure. **Stress Die for anyone engaging with Albrecht.**

A grog who wants to smell the lie — **Perception + Folk Ken vs. Ease Factor 9, Stress Die.** Walk
through the whole mechanic:

- Roll d10. If you roll a **1**: reroll and double! (Show what a spectacular read looks like — the
  grog catches the slight catch in Albrecht's voice, the way his fingers tighten on the document.
  He's absolutely bluffing.)
- If you roll a **0**: hold up — that might be a botch. Roll a **botch die** (another d10). If the
  botch die is also 0: the grog badly misreads the situation — he decides Albrecht is legitimate,
  apologizes for the delay, and tells him the covenant's steward will be happy to discuss boundary
  questions. Albrecht smiles. Heinrich and Kaspar relax. The grogs have just made the situation
  substantially worse.
- Normal success (≥ 9): the grog knows Albrecht is lying, but can't prove it. He can bluff back.
- Failure (< 9): the grog isn't sure either way and the situation is unresolved.

**The Grizzled Veteran's Leadership roll:** if the senior grog wants to shut the conversation
down without committing — "We are on covenant business; the boundary is not our department; speak
to the steward" — that is a **Presence + Leadership vs. Ease Factor 6, Stress Die** (there's
pressure from Heinrich and Kaspar's watchful hands near their belts). Success gets the grogs past
without incident. Failure (or botch) means Albrecht insists on accompanying them back to the
covenant. (He will be a problem there, but that is a later problem — and arguably a bigger one for
Corvus than for the grogs.)

Either way, the grogs get past. They are now behind schedule, and the willow's root-circle was
warmer than it should have been.

> **Rule taught:** The Stress Die's full mechanics — explosion, botch die, botch. The botch
> here doesn't kill anyone but it changes the political situation. Show how a bad die shapes a story.

### Scene 4 — The Ambush at the Culvert
**Rule cluster taught: combat**

Two hundred paces short of the covenant gate, where the path crosses Aldric's drainage culvert
(a timber-and-stone channel that carries marsh water under the path to the brook below), three
men step out of the tree-line. They are not Heinrich and Kaspar.

They are **claim-jumpers** — prospectors hired by a rival Freiburg faction to jump any survey
Albrecht files before the guild can register it. They have been watching the valley for a week.
They saw the grogs at the willow with a sample bag (that's what it looks like to them), and they
want whatever ore sample it contains. They are not professional soldiers. They are desperate men
with belt-knives and staffs, and they will take the satchel by force if they must.

**Run the full combat sequence once, slowly.**

**Step 1 — Initiative**
Everyone rolls: Quickness + weapon modifier + Stress Die. Write down the order from highest to
lowest. The grogs will almost certainly go first — use this to show them the Initiative line on
their cards.

**Step 2 — Attack and Defense**
On each combatant's turn: the attacker rolls **Dexterity + Weapon Ability + weapon mod + Stress Die.**
The defender rolls **Quickness + Weapon Ability + weapon mod + Stress Die.** Subtract Defense from
Attack. If positive, that number is the **Attack Advantage.**

**Step 3 — Damage and Wounds**
Attack Advantage + weapon Damage bonus - defender's Soak = damage total. Look up on the wound ranges:
- 1–5: Light Wound (−1 to everything)
- 6–10: Medium Wound (−3 to everything)
- 11–15: Heavy Wound (−5 to everything)
- 16–20: Incapacitated (unconscious, needs help)
- 21+: potentially fatal (Storyguide's call)

**Step 4 — Fatigue**
If a grog takes an exerting action (sprinting, all-out attack, casting — magic is not happening
here, but strenuous fighting counts), tick one Fatigue level. Penalty applies to all rolls.

**Let the Berserker do something heroically stupid.** If the Berserker charges in: his Attack
+13 is terrifying, but his Defense +7 against three men is survivable only if the others help.
Let him take a Light Wound on a bad roll. This is fine. The session needs blood.

**The claim-jumpers break** when two of them are wounded or one is incapacitated. They are
not soldiers. They scatter into the trees. The grogs do not pursue — the covenant gate is right
there, and they have a flask to deliver.

> **Claim-jumper stats:** Init +1, Attack +5, Defense +3, Damage +4 (knife or staff). Soak +1
> (padded gambeson at best). Wounds: −1 (1–5), −3 (6–10), −5 (11–15), Incapacitated (16–20).
> Run them as a loose group; break morale when two are down.

> **Rule taught:** The complete combat sequence. Show Initiative, Attack vs. Defense, Attack
> Advantage, Damage minus Soak, Wound ranges, and Fatigue. One grog should take a wound.
> Establish that Ars Magica combat is fast and lethal and the wound modifiers matter.

### Scene 5 — What Grows in the Dark
**Rule cluster taught: the world is bigger than them**

The grogs get back to the covenant gate, bloodied and late, and knock. They wait longer than they
should. When the gate opens, it is not a gatekeeper — it is **Aldric of Flambeau**, which means
the grogs have to look at him without flinching, which none of them entirely manage. He is looking
past them, toward the south end of the meadow, where the willow is.

From here, at dusk, they can all see it clearly: the willow is larger than it was this morning.
Noticeably larger. Its canopy has spread. The root-circle that was five paces across is now eight.
And below the roots, where Aldric's drainage culvert runs, the water is backing up — the culvert is
blocked. The new meadow is flooding back.

Aldric says nothing for a long moment. Then:
> *"Give the flask to Konstantinos. He is in the tower. Do not go near the south meadow after
> dark."*

He goes back inside. The gate starts to close.

If a grog pushes — "What is it? What's under the meadow?" — Aldric stops. He considers them.
He says, with the flat patience of a man explaining arithmetic to someone very slow:
> *"Something that was asleep. The water kept it asleep. We drained the water."*

Gate.

**If the Berserker tries to go back and chop down the willow tonight:** The Hunter — highest
Perception, knows when the woods are wrong — notices that the wrong-green circle around the willow
is now visibly pulsing, very faintly, with its own cold light. Not firefly-glow. Stone-deep, slow,
rhythmic, like a heartbeat. The Berserker does not chop down the tree. Nobody goes near the south
meadow. The grogs sleep in the barracks with their boots on.

In the tower, Konstantinos's light burns all night.

> **Rule taught (lightly):** The four Realms — Magic, Faerie, Divine, Infernal — exist and are
> dangerous. The magi caused this; the magi will deal with it, or not; and the grogs will live
> with the consequences either way. This is the wizards' department. The session ends here.

**Total runtime:** One evening. By the end, the players have rolled the Simple Die, the Stress Die,
triggered a botch, run a full combat, and met something they cannot fight — all before learning a
single spell.

## Part 3 — Growing It Into a Saga

The one-shot's ending is your campaign seed. Here is the on-ramp.

**Session 0 — build the central characters.** Now each player makes a **magus** using one of the
House templates *and* a **companion** (a steward, a knight, a wandering scholar). The grogs they
played tonight become the covenant's **pooled** supporting cast — shared, ongoing, and now familiar.

**Adopt troupe-style play.** Each session one player runs their magus, another their companion,
and the rest grab pooled grogs. Grog death remains possible and occasionally glorious.

**Run the calendar, not just the dungeon.** Between adventures, characters spend **seasons**
studying, building labs, inventing spells, aging. Adventures are interruptions. The Silberbachtal
valley changes season by season, and the players author those changes.

**The three threads the one-shot leaves open:**

1. **The Regio.** What is under the meadow? Something old enough to have been there when the marsh
   was young. The magi want its vis. The thing — whatever it is — has not decided yet what it wants
   with them. This can grow into a faerie court, a magical covenant-ruin, or something stranger.

2. **The Merchants.** Albrecht's survey will be filed. The Freiburg guild will push back. Corvus's
   manipulation of Reeve Eckard will eventually become visible to someone with authority. And the
   claim-jumpers were hired by someone who knows the valley has silver — someone who is not going away.

3. **The Prior's Letters.** The Benedictine prior at the ridge-top priory is writing to his abbot.
   The abbot is writing to the bishop. The bishop's secretary was educated in canon law and is aware
   of the Lex Magicae provisions. At some point, an official of the Church will arrive in the valley
   with a questionnaire. The Code of Hermes says *do not endanger the Order with mundanes.* The
   magi's valley is conspicuously, structurally, theologically suspicious blessed.

**The Code tension in one line:** Everything the magi have built is exactly as wonderful and
exactly as illegal as it looks — and the grogs who serve them will be on the wrong side of that
reckoning whether they understand the law or not.
