## How to Read These Cards

Every combat line already bundles the weapon's modifiers. To act:
- **Init / Attack / Defense:** add a Stress Die to the listed number.
- **Damage:** add a Stress Die, then subtract the target's **Soak**; look up the result on the
  wound range.
- **Soak:** listed as a flat number (armor + Stamina). The target adds nothing extra to Soak —
  it is not rolled in standard combat. (Optional: advanced play rolls Stamina for Soak; skip this
  on a first night.)
- **Fatigue Levels:** tick down from OK. Strenuous exertion (sprinting, multiple attacks,
  wrestling) costs a level. The parenthetical penalties on the Wound line are separate — those
  come from injuries, not tiredness.
- **Botch dice:** if you roll a Stress Die and get 0, roll that many extra d10s — one per botch
  die listed for the situation. If any of those is also 0, something goes wrong.

### Brother Albrecht — Freiburg Guild Assayer
*Scene 3 social antagonist; not a fighter*

**Characteristics:** Int +2, Per +2, Pre +1, Com +2, Str 0, Sta 0, Dex 0, Qik −1

**Personality:** Careful +3, Ambitious +2, Bluffing (this scene) +2

**Relevant Abilities:** Folk Ken 4 (merchants), Guile 4 (confident bluffing), Latin 3
(legal documents), Civil & Canon Law 2 (survey rights)

**He is not armed beyond a belt knife and is not looking for a fight.** His goal is to get
access to the valley's eastern ridge for his survey. The fraudulent survey-right document is his
main weapon. He will bluff as long as it works and withdraw if pressed hard by an armed group.

**Detecting the lie:** Per + Folk Ken vs. Ease Factor 9 (Stress Die — social pressure).
Success: the grog reads the slightly too-smooth delivery of a practiced liar.
Botch: the grog accepts the document as legitimate and the situation becomes messier.

### The Claim-Jumpers — Three Desperate Prospectors
*Scene 4 combat antagonists*

Road-thieves and hired jumpers, not soldiers — they have belt-knives, staffs, and fear of
consequences. They believe the satchel contains ore samples from the silver strike. They will
take it by force if they think they can. They will not die for it.

**Combat (all three use this line):**
- *Knife or Staff* — Init +1, Attack +5, Defense +3, Damage +4
- **Soak:** +1 (heavy clothing, no real armor) · Size 0
- **Wounds:** −1 (1–5) · −3 (6–10) · −5 (11–15) · Incap (16–20)

**Morale:** They break and scatter when two of their number are wounded or one is
incapacitated. They are not soldiers. Run them as a loose group and describe one going down
dramatically when the Berserker connects. Their vanguard (the boldest one, who steps out of
the tree-line first) is the trigger for the whole combat; if he goes down early, the other
two need a **Brave check** (Stress Die, roll only Brave trait, vs. Ease Factor 6) to stay
in the fight.

**Botch dice:** If the path is slick and muddy (it rained last night — it is), a botch on
any combat roll outdoors means the character slips. A slipping claim-jumper goes prone and
loses their next action. A slipping grog is just embarrassing, unless they're next to the
drainage culvert, which is a four-foot drop onto stone.

## The Willow
*Not a combatant — a presence*

No stat block. The willow cannot be fought and should not be approached after dark by grogs.
It is the covenant's problem — or rather, it is Aldric's problem, and Aldric will deal with
it in his own time. The grogs' job is to know it is there and stay away from it.

**What Liesel sees at dusk:** Per + Awareness vs. Ease Factor 6 (Simple Die — just looking
in the right direction). Success: the root-circle is larger than it was this morning, and
there is a cold, slow, rhythmic pulse of pale light under the roots. Not fire. Not torchlight.
Something old and not quite aware of anything as recent as them.

> The willow is the scenario's hook into the full saga. Do not explain it tonight.
> Let it breathe.
