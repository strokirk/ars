# The Steward's Errand
### A First-Session Grog Tutorial — *Opponents of the Silbersturz*

---

## What This Is

This tutorial runs **one evening, all grogs** — the outsiders. The players are not the covenant's
servants or heroes. They are Lord Baldewin's men, sent to investigate a valley that has been
producing impossible abundance for three years. They will discover that the tower is real, the
magic is real, and they are entirely out of their depth. They will flee. That's the win condition.

The mighty magi of Silbersturz stay off-stage until the last scene, when one terrible encounter
with their magic sends the grogs running — which teaches, gently, the most important lesson Ars
Magica has: *there are things in this world that swords cannot solve.*

**Each scene teaches one rule cluster, in the same order as a first session should.** Don't front-load
the rules. Let the dice come up when the scene demands them.

---

## Part 1 — What to Prep (the index cards)

Four index cards. That's everything.

**Card 1 — The Mission.**
*"Lord Baldewin's steward Werner has sent you to the Wachteltal — the Quail Valley, three days'
ride southeast. A tower there has been claimed by persons unknown. They have a mill. They have
silver. They have farmland that should be marsh. They pay no lord, no tithe, no tax. Werner wants:
who owns it, under what law, and what it is worth. You are NOT to provoke a confrontation. Return
with information."*
This is the players' charter. They know what Werner wants. They do not know what they're walking into.

**Card 2 — The Valley.**
The Wachteltal is a narrow limestone gorge. The river Wachtel runs north through its center. A
converted Roman tower stands on the western spur, high above the valley floor. The village of
Rohrbeck (forty souls) sits at the valley's narrow throat. The eastern hillside shows ore-spoil
and three mine shafts. The lower valley, which your oldest maps show as marsh, is now black
farmland with a mill at its center. The mill is working. The fields smell wrong — too green,
too heavy with grain, too even in their growth.

**Card 3 — Three Questions Werner Wants Answered.**
1. Who is the legal holder of the tower? (Mundane or Church or something else?)
2. Where does the silver come from, and at what volume?
3. Is there a feudal obligation that Baldewin can attach to?
These questions drive every scene. The grogs are an investigation, not a raid.

**Card 4 — The Warning Signs (for your eyes only).**
- The grain is too perfect and nobody can explain why.
- Reeve Dietrich deflects every question about the tower's occupants.
- The mill-keeper's wife called Sergeant Harbert by name before he introduced himself.
- The man by the mill dam makes the air feel wrong.

---

## The Only Dice Rule (read this aloud)

> Almost everything is **Characteristic + Ability + a d10, versus an Ease Factor.** Meet or beat
> it, you succeed.
>
> - **Simple Die:** roll a d10, 0 = 10. Used when calm.
> - **Stress Die:** roll a d10, but **1 explodes** (reroll and double, again on another 1), and
>   **0 means danger** — roll a botch die; if it shows 0, something goes badly wrong. Used whenever
>   there is pressure.
>
> Ease Factors: **3** = simple, **6** = easy, **9** = average, **12** = hard, **15** = very hard.

Introduce these one at a time, as the scenes demand. Not all at once. Never all at once.

## Part 2 — The One-Shot: *"The Steward's Errand"*

### Scene 1 — The Muster at Baldewin's Gate *(teaches: the character sheet)*

Werner lines the grogs up in the courtyard at first light. He's a dry, efficient man. He doesn't
like sending soldiers on accounting missions, but he is a conscientious steward.

Go around the table: each player reads their grog's name, one Personality Trait, and their best
Ability aloud. Have Werner react to each one — a nod to the veteran, a long look at the berserker,
a clipped "you'll do" to the scout. This is scene-setting, not dice-rolling.

**Werner's final words:** *"Ride to Rohrbeck. Talk to the reeve — a man called Dietrich. Find out
who is in that tower, what law they claim, and what the silver and mill are worth. Do not threaten
anyone. Do not enter the tower uninvited. Come back."*

What Werner does not say: he has heard the priest's letters. He does not believe in witches. He
is, quietly, beginning to wonder.

> **Rule taught:** What's on the sheet. *Characteristics* are who you are; *Abilities* are what
> you've trained. No dice yet — orient the players to their cards and to one another.

---

### Scene 2 — Rohrbeck *(teaches: the Simple Die and Ease Factors)*

The grogs arrive in Rohrbeck at midday. The village is odd in a way that takes a moment to name:
it is too well-fed. The children are not thin. The dogs are not ribs and fur. The granary is full
in June, which is the hungry month. The fields beyond the village are green and heavy.

Reeve Dietrich receives them in his hall. He is cooperative, careful, and evasive in the way that
a man is evasive when he is telling the truth but knows the truth is strange. He confirms:

- The tower is occupied by "scholars" — three men, he thinks, with servants.
- They have been here three years.
- The marsh was drained by "labor" — he gestures vaguely toward the eastern slope.
- The silver comes from the old Roman shafts; the occupants reopened them.
- He does not think they have a feudal grant. He thinks it is "a matter for the Church, perhaps."
  He says this quickly and then does not elaborate.

**Skill checks here (Simple Die — no pressure, just legwork):**

- **Folk Ken** vs. Ease 9: Dietrich is telling the truth as he understands it, but he is afraid.
  He deflects every question about the occupants personally — he will describe deeds but not men.
- **Awareness** vs. Ease 6: The grain in the fields is not just abundant — it is *even*. Every stalk
  the same height. No bare patches, no weed-choked corners. Like a drawing of a field rather than
  a field.
- **Bargain** or **Charm** vs. Ease 9: Getting Dietrich to name the number of workers or the
  silver output. He "cannot say exactly." With a success he admits the autumn render was sixty
  marks of refined coin. With a failure he smiles and offers them lunch.

> **Rule taught:** The basic roll — Characteristic + Ability + Simple Die vs. Ease Factor. Safe
> situation, no botch risk. Let someone fail and get stonewalled — information denied, not
> catastrophe. Show that Abilities solve problems, not just combat.

### Scene 3 — The Mill *(teaches: the Stress Die and the botch)*

The grogs ride out to the mill in the afternoon. It is a good mill — too good, too new, the
stonework seamless in a way that local masons couldn't achieve. The mill dam is cut stone, perfectly
jointed. The mill-race runs straight as a ruled line.

The mill-keeper's wife, a woman named Ilse, is hanging washing. She looks up when the grogs arrive.
She says: *"You took longer than expected, Sergeant Harbert. My lord thought you'd be here by Tierce."*

She has never met them. Werner's party left Baldewin's gate three days ago, in the other direction.

This is the moment the grogs understand that something knows they were coming. They can react however
their players choose — but **now there's pressure**, and pressure means Stress Dice.

**Skill checks here (Stress Die — social tension, stakes exist):**

- **Folk Ken** vs. Ease 9 (Stress): Reading Ilse. She is not threatening them. She seems
  unsurprised, almost bored. Success: she is a covenfolk — she has lived near the magi long enough
  that their ways are normal to her. Botch: the grog misreads her calm as hostility and reacts —
  Ilse takes three steps back and shouts toward the tower; the captain has sixty seconds to de-escalate.

- **Intrigue** vs. Ease 12 (Stress): Identifying *how* the covenant knew their route and timing.
  A good roll gets the grog to the right answer: someone in Baldewin's court talked, or the
  scholars have ways of listening. A botch: the grog concludes it must be Dietrich, and confronts
  him that evening — wrongly, and loudly, in the village square — creating bad blood with the one
  cooperative local.

- **Awareness** vs. Ease 6 (Stress): Noticing the water in the mill-race. It flows in slow spirals
  where it should rush straight. The patterns are beautiful and wrong. This is Aldric's sigil,
  still in the water from his morning work.

> **Rule taught:** The Stress Die: the 1-explosion and the 0-botch. Let the die do its work.
> If someone botches the Folk Ken, run the confrontation with Ilse — it's a social combat, loud
> and embarrassing, and it teaches that botches change the situation, not just the roll.

### Scene 4 — The Man by the River *(teaches: combat — and the world outside combat)*

Late afternoon. The grogs have walked the mill dam and they want to see the eastern hillside and
the mine shafts. The logical path runs along the river bank, past the mill pond, under the shadow
of the reclaimed fields. Aldric of Flambeau is standing in the mill-race, thigh-deep, examining
something in the current. He is not looking at the grogs.

Run the Gift onset as description, not mechanics. The players don't get a roll — they just feel it:

*"The man in the water has his back to you. He is doing nothing threatening. But the air near him
is wrong in the same way that meat left in the sun is wrong — you know it before you can say why.
Your horse doesn't want to approach the bank. Your feet don't want to carry you closer. Whatever
this man is, he is not like you."*

Aldric does not turn. He does not speak. He has known they were there since they arrived (Konstantinos
told him). He is waiting to see what they do.

**The grogs have three options:**

**Option A — Back off.** Smart. They note his existence and go around. They reach the mine shafts,
examine the ore spoil-heap (well-processed, substantial: Awareness vs. Ease 6 to estimate sixty
or seventy marks of output annually), and retreat to Rohrbeck for the night.

**Option B — Hail him or approach.** The Gift intensifies with proximity. Any grog who comes within
twenty paces must roll **Brave + Presence vs. Ease 9** (Stress Die — this matters) or visibly
flinch back. Success means they hold their ground but feel cold. Aldric turns, looks at them for
four seconds — his eyes are entirely calm — and then looks back at the water. He has nothing to
say to them.

**Option C — Aggression.** If any grog raises a weapon (possible with the Berserker), run combat.
The rules are:
- **Initiative** = Quickness + weapon modifier + Stress Die. Rolled once for the fight.
- **Attack** (Dex + Ability + weapon + die) vs. **Defense** (Qik + Ability + weapon + die).
- Beat their Defense? The gap is your **Attack Advantage.** Add it to **Damage**, subtract **Soak**.
- Check the **wound range** for Light / Medium / Heavy / Incapacitating.

In this case: Aldric is a magus. He has Parma Magica (treat his Magic Resistance as a Soak of +20
against all weapons; attacks simply don't land). Every blow the grogs strike bounces or deflects,
and Aldric still doesn't turn around. After three rounds of this he steps out of the mill-race and
walks toward the tower. He does not run. He does not hurry.

> **Rule taught (primary):** Combat — initiative, attack vs. defense, damage, wounds. Run through
> it once fully and slowly even if Option C doesn't happen — have a covenfolk gate-guard intercept
> them at the mine spoil-heap and run a brief exchange to teach the sequence. He can be backed down
> (Folk Ken or Leadership vs. Ease 9) or fought (he's cautious, not suicidal: see cast cards).
> **Secondary lesson (Option B/C):** There is something in this world that combat does not resolve.
> The gate-guard fight is the rules tutorial. Aldric is the first sign that this is a different
> category of problem.

### Scene 5 — The Wave *(teaches: the world is bigger than them)*

That night, camped outside Rohrbeck, the grogs have what they need to report. The smart play is to
leave at dawn. But the valley has one more thing to show them.

At midnight, someone screams from the mine shafts. When the grogs investigate — torches, weapons
out, Stress Dice rolling for awareness and movement in the dark — they find the eastern hillside
has *moved*. The three mine-shaft entrances are sealed under a fresh earthen ridge, smooth as
plaster, still steaming faintly. Corvus has collapsed the shafts.

Not because the grogs are there. Because Konstantinos told him the Speyer filing was rejected —
the count's clerk has sent a writ to investigate — and Corvus's contingency plan for exactly this
situation was always to remove the evidence. The grogs walked into the middle of a planned
operation.

As they stand at the sealed shafts, the hillside shudders. Then it shudders again. Then, from above
them — thirty yards up the slope, standing in moonlight — Corvus. He has a lantern in one hand.
He is watching them with academic patience.

He says: *"You should leave now. I would prefer not to do this twice."*

The ground has a voice. Tell the players what a Crest of the Earth Wave feels like from the
wrong end: a low grinding sound, then the soil rises in a smooth swell three feet high, moving
outward from Corvus's feet like a wave in deep water, crossing forty feet of slope in three seconds,
passing under the grogs' feet and continuing toward the valley floor. Nobody is killed. Everyone
is knocked down, and the torches go out.

When the grogs find their feet again, Corvus is gone. The slope is quiet. The sealed shafts are
still sealed.

There is nothing here that swords fix. The valley's abundance, the drained marsh, the bent reeve,
the silver, the impossible knowledge of their arrival — all of it belongs to the men in that tower,
and one of them just moved a hillside to underscore the point.

**They run.** Back to Rohrbeck, horses, the road north, three days to Baldewin's gate. The Hunter
is the last one off the slope, watching the tower's windows, where a single lamp burns.

> **Rule taught (lightly):** The Realms of Power — Magic exists and operates outside combat's
> grammar. Magi are not harder enemies; they are a different category of problem. The grogs are
> not cowards for running. They are correct. Hook planted.

## Part 3 — Growing It Into a Saga

The one-shot's last image is your campaign seed: Werner's report, laid on Baldewin's table, which
reads something like *"tower occupied by practitioners of unlawful art, silver sealed, water-rights
claim filed, all mundane leverage removed, recommend Church consultation."* Werner writes "Church"
because he doesn't know the word "Hermetic."

That report is now in play. Here is where it goes:

**Session 0 — Build the central characters.** Players may now make magi from a rival covenant or
a Quaesitor and her entourage, or play as Baldewin's more capable agents — a knight, a Church
scholar, a hedge wizard. The grogs they just played become the campaign's recurring pooled cast.
Some of them have now *met* Aldric at the mill-race. They will not forget it.

**The escalation arc writes itself:**

- **Baldewin consults the bishop.** Father Gebhard's letters are on the bishop's desk. The bishop
  sends a canon lawyer to the Wachteltal. He doesn't come back, not because he was hurt, but
  because Konstantinos had a conversation with him that the canon lawyer found more compelling than
  his original instructions. The bishop is now alarmed.

- **Silbersturz is noticed by the Rhine Tribunal.** A Quaesitor named Margarethe — senior,
  politically cautious — begins assembling evidence. She will not move until she has five clear
  violations, because she knows Corvus is Tytalus and will litigate every one. She is at three.

- **Corvus makes his mistake.** He tries to bend Baldewin directly using *Aura of Rightful Authority*.
  A companion-class character in Baldewin's retinue has Magic Resistance (a hedge wizard, an
  ordained priest with True Faith, or a formerly-graced person). The spell fails. Baldewin's
  household now knows for certain what they're dealing with. The theological problem becomes the
  existential problem.

- **The covenant fractures.** Aldric wants to flood the road north and be done with it. Konstantinos
  wants to monitor and manage. Corvus wants to negotiate. They are not agreed. This is the players'
  opening — find the crack and push.

The grogs' players have already been inside the valley. They know the layout. They know Aldric
stands at the mill in the afternoons. They know Ilse the mill-keeper's wife will look at them with
recognition. They have a map, a fear, and a reason.

> **The whole pedagogy in one line:** start as the small people, feel how large the other side is,
> then earn the tools to fight back.
