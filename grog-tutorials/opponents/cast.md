# Cast Cards — *The Steward's Errand*

All cards needed to run the session. Player characters first (hand these out), then NPCs and
opponents. Combat lines have weapon modifiers pre-bundled — just add the Stress Die.

**How to read a card fast:**
- **Init / Attack / Defense** — add a Stress Die to the listed number.
- **Damage** — add a Stress Die result, then subtract the target's Soak; check the wound range.
- **Soak** — subtract from incoming damage (no die — it's a flat buffer).
- **Fatigue Levels** — tick down each time the grog over-exerts. Wound penalties are separate.

## Player Characters — Baldewin's Grogs

These are Werner's men: Lord Baldewin's soldiers, servants of a minor count's vassal, entirely
mundane. They are competent at what soldiers do. That is not the right toolkit for this job, and
learning that is the session's lesson.

## NPCs — Rohrbeck and the Valley

---

### Reeve Dietrich — *"A thoroughly bent man"*

Not an enemy. Not an ally. A small official who has been shown that cooperation with the tower
makes his life better in every material sense, and who has also, once, watched Corvus demonstrate
what happens to earth when you push it wrong. He talks. He deflects. He is fundamentally honest
within the narrow space Corvus left him.

**Use for:** Scene 2 questioning. He answers truthfully but shallowly. He will not volunteer
information about the magi personally. He does not lie outright — he avoids.

**Folk Ken vs. Ease 9:** He is afraid, not conspiratorial. He is protecting himself, not the
covenant. He would cooperate with an authority he believed could protect him afterward. None is
currently available.

---

### Ilse — *"The mill-keeper's wife"*

Covenfolk. Has lived in Silbersturz's shadow for three years. The Gift doesn't affect her anymore
— proximity to magi builds tolerance among those who work for them. She is used to the spiraling
water, the too-even fields, the man who watches from the tower at dawn.

She called Harbert by name because Konstantinos mentioned it at supper two nights ago after
listening to the Speyer merchant's dreams. She doesn't know how he knew. She stopped asking.

**Use for:** Scene 3's shock moment. She is not threatening; she is simply part of a household
where being known from a distance is ordinary.

## Magi (Off-Stage Reference)

The magi do not receive stat cards because they do not fight the grogs directly. What the
Storyguide needs:

**Aldric — Scene 4 mill-dam encounter:**
- Blatant Gift: Brave + Presence vs. Ease 9 (Stress) to approach within 20 paces without flinching.
- Parma Magica: treat as flat Soak +20 against all physical attacks. Weapons do not penetrate.
  Describe this as blows deflecting, sliding, or simply stopping before contact — not bouncing
  (that would imply a visible effect; Parma is invisible).
- *Mighty Torrent of Water* (CrAq 20): if the grogs don't retreat, a 5-foot-wide jet of river
  water hits at Initiative +4, deals +10 Damage (no attack roll — it's a targeted area effect;
  victim rolls Quickness + Athletics vs. Ease 12 to partly dodge, halving damage on success). This
  is used once, at full force, as a definitive warning. Aldric does not pursue.

**Corvus — Scene 5 hillside:**
- *Crest of the Earth Wave* (ReTe 20): A 15-foot swell of earth rolls outward from his feet,
  crossing 40 yards of slope. Everyone in its path rolls Quickness + Athletics vs. Ease 9 (Stress)
  or is knocked prone and loses their torch. No damage. Pure intimidation — the ground moved. The
  grogs are not the target. Corvus collapsed the shafts before they arrived; this is a warning, not
  an execution.

**Konstantinos — Invisible throughout:**
- He is watching via *The Ear for Distant Voices* (InIm 20) from the beginning. He is not present
  physically. The only sign of him is Ilse knowing Harbert's name, and — if the Storyguide wants
  a late beat — a scrying window that the Scout's Per +3 might notice in Scene 5: a brief shimmer
  in the air near the sealed shaft, roughly eye-height, like heat haze in the cold night. Awareness
  vs. Ease 12. If she makes it, she sees a circle of light the size of a dinner plate hanging in
  the air, watching them. It vanishes when she looks directly at it.
