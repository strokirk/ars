# Covenant of the Silbersturz
### The Antagonist Covenant

---

## The Place

**Silbersturz** — "Silverfall" in the local dialect — sits in the **Wachteltal**, the Quail Valley,
a narrow limestone gorge cut by the River Wachtel in the foothills east of the Rhine, three days'
ride south of Mainz. The valley was nobody's prize before the covenant arrived: a wedge of marginal
farmland, a reedy marsh in the flood-plain, a village of forty souls called Rohrbeck, and an old
Roman prospecting scar on the eastern hillside that had been producing dribbles of silver for two
centuries without ever yielding a proper vein.

The covenant itself occupies a converted Roman watchtower and bath-complex on the valley's high
western spur. The magi sealed and rebuilt the old hypocaust tunnels as a laboratory basement; the
upper tower has been extended in dark local limestone. From the valley floor it looks imposing
enough, but the real marker is the landscape around it: the marsh that once swallowed the lower
third of the valley is gone. In its place, dark loamy fields push right to the river's new,
stone-banked channel. A mill pond sits where reeds stood three years ago. Every field in Rohrbeck
looks obscenely healthy.

**Mood:** Coiled ambition behind a pastoral face. The valley is prosperous, well-ordered, and
quietly strange. The peasants of Rohrbeck are fed and alive, and they know it, and they are afraid
of the men on the hill in the way people are afraid of gods who have been kind so far. Outsiders
who arrive in a hungry year and see the harvest find it unsettling before they find it enviable.
The place feels *managed* — not by a lord, not by God, but by something that has decided to use
this valley as a workshop.

## The Three Magi

### Corvus of Tytalus
*"Old Crow"*

**Sigil:** His magic carries the faint smell of turned earth, of a freshly-dug grave.

**Quirk:** Corvus is always building something in his head. He speaks in proposals and counterproposals,
never conclusions. Every conversation is an Intrigue roll he is running privately. He will offer
you a deal before he has decided whether he wants you alive to keep it.

**Current scheme:** Corvus has identified the valley's reeve, a man named Dietrich, as the critical
mundane lever. Three months ago Corvus cast *Aura of Rightful Authority* on Dietrich and suggested
that the covenant's water-rights claim on the full length of the Wachtel River was "obviously
established by ancient grant." Dietrich now believes this with his whole heart and has filed the
claim with the count's court at Speyer. Corvus is waiting to see whether the count's clerk will
accept the claim quietly or whether a negotiation — of a more personal kind — will be required.

**Role in the scenario:** Off-stage entirely until Scene 5. He is the reason the outsiders were sent
— he bent Reeve Dietrich, and Dietrich's behavior is what alerted Lord Baldewin's steward. In
Scene 5, if the grogs penetrate deep enough into the valley, they may glimpse the result of his
earth-work: the banked river channel, the drained marsh, the silver-ore spoil-heap at the hill's
base — and hear the crack of *Crest of the Earth Wave* as the hillside warns them off.

**Signature magic (used this scenario):** *Crest of the Earth Wave* (ReTe 20) — a 15-foot swell
of earth that rolls outward from a struck point, knocking men from their feet and cracking masonry.
Used as a warning shot, not a killing blow. The ground *moves* and they will understand they are not
dealing with a lord or a bandit.

### Konstantinos of Bonisagus
*"The Lamp-Eater" (his covenfolk call him this; he doesn't know)*

**Sigil:** His magic leaves everything it touches slightly too clear. Shadows fall at the wrong angle.
Colors are marginally too saturated. It lasts only moments, but in that moment the world looks like
a manuscript illumination, not real life.

**Quirk:** Konstantinos keeps a wax tablet on his person at all times and writes down everything
he learns — not for memory (his memory is excellent) but because the act of recording it feels like
ownership. He writes notes about other people's faces when he thinks they can't see him.

**Current scheme:** Konstantinos has been running a scrying web for seven months. He cast *The Ear
for Distant Voices* and *Posing the Silent Question* on a traveling merchant from Rohrbeck who
visits Speyer's market quarterly — a "mundane relay" who doesn't know he's been questioned while
sleeping. Through this relay Konstantinos has tracked the growing unease in Lord Baldewin's court:
the count's clerk who received Dietrich's water-rights filing, a steward who mentioned "the witches'
valley" at a feast, a serjeant who asked too many questions about Rohrbeck's harvest last autumn.
He knew this delegation was coming before they left Baldewin's gate. He told the other two. Corvus
said "good — let them come and see the mill." Aldric said nothing, which is its own answer.

**Role in the scenario:** The invisible eye. Konstantinos is watching the grogs' approach from the
moment they enter the valley. The players won't know this until Scene 4, when something reveals
that the covenant knew their route, their number, and their leader's name before they announced
themselves. A covenfolk woman at the mill calls their captain "Sergeant Harbert" without having
been told. That's Konstantinos.

**Signature magic (used this scenario):** *Sight of the Active Magics* (InVi 40) and *The Ear for
Distant Voices* (InIm 20). He watches. He listens. He does not interfere directly — he doesn't
need to.

### Aldric of Flambeau
*"The Drainer"*

**Sigil:** Water he has touched won't flow in a straight line for a day. It curls, eddies, spirals
in glass-smooth patterns that look carved. Local people find cups of water left near the mill dam
that hold still geometric shapes on their surfaces at dawn.

**Quirk:** Aldric has the Blatant Gift, which means every mundane who looks at him feels an
instinctive animal wrongness — not malice, not deformity, just *wrongness*, the way a healthy body
recoils from spoiled meat. He has had this his entire life. He has stopped apologizing for it. He
now finds it clarifying: it means people deal with him honestly, because performance fails in his
presence. He considers this a social advantage.

**Current scheme:** Aldric has been flooding and re-draining the eastern side of the valley for two
growing seasons, methodically reclaiming a strip of former marsh that runs from the river to the
hill's base. Each autumn he pushes the boundary twenty yards further. The new land is in his name —
registered via Reeve Dietrich, who was very helpful — and the yield from last season's rye on the
reclaimed strip was more than double what the old marsh-edge fields produced. Aldric intends to have
the entire flood-plain under cultivation within three years. He is also quietly considering whether
the *next* valley over might benefit from similar attention.

**Blatant Gift effect in play:** Any mundane NPC who sees Aldric within 10 paces must make a
Personality roll (Brave vs. Ease Factor 9) or edge away, avoid eye contact, or find a reason to
leave. Covenfolk are exempt (they have Virtue: Covenfolk, or equivalent acclimation). Grogs who
encounter him should be told: *"Something is wrong with the man by the river. Not his face, not his
posture. Something underneath. You do not want to be here anymore."*

**Role in the scenario:** Aldric is the first and worst magus the grogs actually *see*, and they
see him at a distance, wading in the mill-race, apparently doing nothing. His Gift hits them before
they've decided whether he's an enemy. He does not speak to them. If they come closer, the feeling
intensifies. If they try to cross the mill dam, he turns and faces them — still silent — and they
get the full Blatant Gift plus the impossible smoothness of the water around him. He is the wall
before the wall.

**Signature magic (used this scenario):** *Mighty Torrent of Water* (CrAq 20) — a 5-foot-wide
jet of water with the force of a running river, launched up to 40 paces. If the grogs don't flee
at the mill dam, the river comes to meet them.

## The Seasons of Conspicuous Deeds

The covenant has been in the Wachteltal for three years. Here, in order, is what they have done —
and what each deed has cost them in scrutiny.

**Year 1, Spring — The Draining.** Aldric spent the entire season working the lower marsh.
*Parting the Waves* + *Closing the Earth's Gate* + two months of directed labor. By summer the
reeds were dying and the first drainage ditches were cut in raw clay. The valley people watched.
The village priest told them God was blessing the land. He was nervous when he said it.

**Year 1, Autumn — The Silver Strike.** Corvus walked the eastern hillside with *Probe for Pure
Silver* and then *Miner's Keen Eye* and marked the vein locations with iron stakes. He hired a
team of miners out of Freiberg and paid them in covenant silver to open three adits. By Martinmas
the first ore was coming out. The miners went home. The ore processing stayed. Rohrbeck's smith
was given new tools and a new forge and told to smelt on Fridays. He does. He doesn't ask where
the ore comes from.

**Year 2, Summer — The Mill.** Corvus and Aldric together. Aldric moved the river into its new
stone-banked channel in six days. Corvus raised the millstones with *The Unseen Porter* and
set the mill-race walls with *Rock of Viscid Clay* + *Earth That Breaks No More*. The mill has
been turning since August of last year. Reeve Dietrich collects the toll on behalf of "the tower."
He keeps 20%. The covenant keeps the rest. The valley has never had a mill. The next valley over
sends grain here to be ground. People talk.

**Year 2, Winter — The Bent Reeve.** Corvus took Dietrich aside at the Martinmas feast and cast
*Aura of Rightful Authority*. The conversation lasted an hour. Dietrich emerged convinced that
the ancient water-rights of the valley, by natural and written law, belonged to whoever held the
tower. He filed accordingly at Speyer. The clerk recorded it. Nobody in the count's court has yet
decided how alarmed to be.

**Year 3, Spring (current) — The Scrying Web.** Konstantinos has been running his ear-relay on
the Speyer merchant for two seasons. This is the deed that crosses the hardest line — not Hermetic
but divine, in the Church's accounting, and potentially monstrous in anyone's. He has been reading
a sleeping man's thoughts quarterly. He knows the count's court is moving. He has told no Quaesitor.
He has no intention of doing so.

## The Deed That Sends the Outsiders

It is the silver that tips the scale.

Rohrbeck's autumn yield was visibly, incomprehensibly good — double the manor average in a year
when the Rhine valley's harvests were weak. That alone might have been weather or good management.
But then the silver: Reeve Dietrich paid the count's autumn render in refined coin instead of raw
ore, which he has never done, which means either he has a smelter now or someone is refining for
him. And then the water-rights claim, filed by a reeve who has never previously shown ambition, on
behalf of a tower that has been occupied for three years by persons unknown who are not on any
feudal roll Lord Baldewin can find.

Baldewin's steward, a sharp man named Werner, put three things together: the harvest, the silver,
and the missing feudal obligations. He brought them to his lord. Lord Baldewin — a minor count's
vassal, ambitious, behind on his own renders — sees a demesne operating inside his presumed territory
with no lord, no tithe, and no obligation to anyone. He sent Werner with a company of eight to
investigate, assess, and report.

Werner's orders are: *find out who owns that tower, what law they operate under, and what their
production is worth.* He was not told there might be wizards. Nobody thought to mention it, because
nobody in Baldewin's hall believes the rumors from Rohrbeck's priest.

Werner's company is the player characters.

## A Note on the Theological Problem

The abundance in the Wachteltal is, as the character concept predicts, a theological problem first
and a legal one second. Father Gebhard of Rohrbeck has been writing letters to his bishop in Mainz
for a year. His letters say that the valley is blessed, that the harvest is miraculous, that the
men in the tower are "strange and feared by their own servants, but their works are the works of
God." He means this as praise. His bishop, who is a shrewder man, has not responded but has not
thrown the letters away.

The outsider grogs are in front of this wave, not behind it. If they return to Baldewin with
evidence of magic, the theological problem becomes Baldewin's problem, which becomes the bishop's
problem, which becomes the Order of Hermes' problem — eventually. That escalation is the campaign.
