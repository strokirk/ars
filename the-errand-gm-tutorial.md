# So You Want to Run Ars Magica
### A First-Time Storyguide's Pocket Tutorial

Ars Magica is a game about *wizards* — but the fastest way to crash a first session is to hand four newcomers four world-bending magi and a 200-page magic system. So we don't. This tutorial runs **one evening, all grogs** (the covenant's ordinary soldiers and servants), and teaches the rules in the order the dice actually come up. The mighty magi stay locked in their laboratories, exactly where the setting says they belong. You and your players learn the *world* and the *system* through the eyes of the little people who live in it.

---

## Part 1 — What to Prep (the setting, lightly)

You need **four things on an index card each**, no more:

1. **The Covenant.** The wizards' fortified home and your whole campaign's anchor. Invent a name, a place, a mood — e.g. *"Stonebrook, a half-ruined Roman watchtower in a misty Welsh valley, 1220 AD."* The covenant "is as much a character in the game as any of the human beings" (Ch.1). Players never need its full history tonight — just that it's *home*, and they're sworn to it.

2. **Two off-stage magi.** Names and one quirk each ("Vera, sharp-tongued, obsessed with the stars"; "Old Corvus, never leaves his lab"). The grogs serve them and fear them a little. The magi do **not** appear until the very end, if at all. This teaches the core social truth of the setting: magi are powerful, strange, and *not your friends*.

3. **Mythic Europe in one sentence.** It's the real 13th-century Middle Ages — real France, real Pope — *plus* magic, faeries, demons, and angels that are all literally real. You need no more history than that. If accuracy spoils fun, use less of it (Ch.14 says so explicitly).

4. **The grogs.** Don't build characters — *hand them out*. Use the printed templates (Ch.3): **The Berserker, The Grizzled Veteran, The Hunter, The Standard Soldier, The Specialist.** Give each player one or two. They're disposable, hammy, and fun precisely because nobody is attached to them yet.

That's your entire prep. One covenant, two wizards, one sentence of history, five pregens.

---

## The Only Dice Rule (read this aloud)

> Almost everything is **Characteristic + Ability + a d10, versus an Ease Factor.** Meet or beat it, you succeed.
>
> - **Simple Die:** roll a d10, 0 = 10. Used when calm.
> - **Stress Die:** roll a d10, but **1 explodes** (reroll & double, again if you roll another 1), and **0 means danger** — roll a *botch die*; if it shows 0, something goes badly wrong. Used whenever there's pressure.
>
> Ease Factors: **3** = simple, **6** = easy, **9** = average, **12** = hard, **15** = very hard.

You will introduce these one at a time below. Don't front-load them.

## Part 2 — The One-Shot: *"The Errand"*

The magi have sent their grogs to walk to the village of **Nantcwm**, half a day off, to collect a sealed crate the covenant is owed. Simple. It will not stay simple. Each scene below quietly teaches one rule cluster.

### Scene 1 — The Gate *(teaches: the character sheet)*
The grogs muster in the courtyard at dawn. Go around the table: each player reads their grog's name, one Personality Trait, and their best Ability aloud, in character. The Grizzled Veteran grumbles; the Berserker is already spoiling for a fight.
> **Rule taught:** what's on the sheet. *Characteristics* (Str, Per, Com…) are who you are; *Abilities* (Awareness, Single Weapon…) are what you've trained. That's all. No dice yet — just orient them.

### Scene 2 — The Ford *(teaches: the Simple Die & Ease Factors)*
A rain-swollen stream blocks the trail. Crossing the slick stepping-stones is an **Athletics** check, Ease Factor 6. No danger, so they roll the **Simple Die**: Quickness + Athletics + d10 ≥ 6.
> **Rule taught:** the basic roll, and that *easy + safe = Simple Die, no botch.* Let someone fail and get a soaking — consequences without stakes. Then let the Hunter recall the safer crossing (an **Area Lore** check) to show Abilities solve problems, not just muscles.

### Scene 3 — The Villager's Lie *(teaches: the Stress Die & the botch)*
In Nantcwm the headman is shifty: the crate "was already collected." Someone's lying. A grog tries to read him — **Folk Ken** or **Guile**, Ease Factor 9 — but now there's social pressure, so it's a **Stress Die**. Explain the explosion (roll a 1!) and the botch (roll a 0, then a botch die). A botch here means the grog accuses the wrong man and the room turns hostile.
> **Rule taught:** stress, spectacular success, and catastrophic failure. This is the heartbeat of Ars Magica's dice. The crate, it turns out, was stolen on the road by bandits.

### Scene 4 — The Ambush *(teaches: combat)*
On the way back, the bandits spring. **Run the full sequence once, slowly:**
- **Initiative** = Quickness + weapon mod + Stress Die. Rolled once for the fight.
- **Attack** (Dex + Ability + weapon + die) vs. **Defense** (Qik + Ability + weapon + die).
- Beat their Defense? The gap is your **Attack Advantage.** Add it to **Damage**, subtract their **Soak.** Cross-reference the **Wound table** for Light/Medium/Heavy/Incapacitating.
- Introduce **Fatigue** (running, exertion) and let a grog tire.
> **Rule taught:** combat is *fast and deadly.* A single botched Defense can kill a grog outright — which is exactly the lesson. Let the Berserker do something heroically stupid. If a grog dies, mourn him fondly and move on. That's the genre.

### Scene 5 — Something in the Woods *(teaches: the world is bigger than them)*
The bandits flee toward a barrow that *hums wrong* — the air tastes of magic (or faerie, or something infernal). The grogs are out of their depth, and they know it. They grab the crate and run. Back at Stonebrook, a magus finally descends from her tower, takes the crate without thanks, and murmurs something about "a regio waking up."
> **Rule taught (lightly):** the supernatural Realms — Magic, Faerie, Divine, Infernal — exist and are dangerous, and *this is the wizards' department, not yours.* Hook planted. Session ends.

**Total runtime:** one evening. By the end, players have used the Simple Die, the Stress Die, a botch, a full combat, and met the world — without ever reading a spell.

## Part 3 — Growing It Into a Saga (≈10 sessions)

The one-shot's final scene is your campaign seed. Here's the on-ramp:

- **Session 0 — build the central characters.** Now each player makes a **magus** (one of the House templates) *and* a **companion** (a knight, scholar, witch…). The grogs they already played become the covenant's **pooled** supporting cast — shared, ongoing, and now familiar.

- **Adopt troupe-style play.** This is the heart of an Ars saga (Ch.16). Each session, one player runs their magus, another their companion, and the rest grab pooled grogs. The number of magi on any adventure stays small (easier for you to challenge), everyone gets the spotlight over time, and *grogs are still allowed to die.* The Storyguide hat can even pass around the table for guest sessions.

- **Run the calendar, not just the dungeon.** Between adventures, characters spend **seasons** studying, building labs, inventing spells, aging. Adventures are "occasional interruptions in their lives," not a weekly treadmill. This is what makes Ars Magica feel like a chronicle rather than a campaign.

- **The 10-session arc writes itself from Scene 5:** that waking *regio* by the barrow becomes a faerie or magical site the covenant must investigate, exploit for **vis** (raw magic), and defend — drawing in a rival covenant, a suspicious bishop, and the Order of Hermes' own politics. The grogs your players first loved get maimed, promoted, and remembered, while the magi they've now built rise to meet threats no soldier could.

> **The whole pedagogy in one line:** start small, mortal, and disposable — earn the magic.

## Appendix — Five Run-Ready Grogs

Hand these out as-is. Each line a player needs in *The Errand* is here. To resolve anything: **Characteristic + Ability + die ≥ Ease Factor.** Combat lines already bundle weapon modifiers — just add the Stress Die.

**How to read a card fast:**
- **Init / Attack / Defense** — add a Stress Die to the listed number.
- **Damage** — add a Stress Die, then subtract the target's Soak; check the wound range.
- **Soak** — add a Stress Die when *you* are hit.
- **Fatigue Levels** — tick down from OK each time the grog over-exerts; the parenthetical penalties on the *Wound* line are separate, from injuries.

### Bandits (Scene 4 — quick stats for ~4 enemies)
Cowardly road-thieves in rags, not soldiers. Run them as one **Untrained Group** using the vanguard's line; they break and flee once two of them drop or their vanguard is wounded.
- *Spear or club* — Init +1, Attack +6, Defense +4, Damage +5
- **Soak:** +2 (leather scraps) · **Size 0**
- **Wounds:** −1 (1–5), −3 (6–10), −5 (11–15), Incapacitated (16–20)
> *Tuned so a competent grog wins but a botched Defense still draws blood. If the table is cruising, add a botch die for the rain and mud to show how conditions raise the stakes.*
